# Website, X, team, community, and compromise risk

Use for identity, social authenticity, community quality, impersonation, account compromise, and rug risk.

## Cross-link evidence

Map website, X, docs, GitHub, exact CA, treasury, explorer metadata, launchpad/issuer, and named-team profiles. Reciprocal links created at the same time are weaker than older independent attestations. A CA in an X bio proves only that the current operator placed it there.

Check domain age/redirects, copied text, broken docs, placeholder policies, wallet-drainer behavior, archived pages, and whether claims have contract evidence. Registrar privacy alone is not guilt.

## Freshness and AI-template launch risk

Build a dated launch timeline whenever the project is new or rug risk is part of the question:

- domain registration/creation date and, separately, earliest archived or independently indexed site appearance;
- X account creation date, previous identity if any, and date of the first project-relevant post;
- token contract deployment, first mint/distribution, pool creation, and first trade;
- first independent mention, first usable product evidence, and first verifiable revenue or user activity.

Do not confuse account age with project history. An old dormant or renamed X account that began posting about the project only recently provides little history and may be purchased, recycled, or compromised. State exact dates and the observation timezone; if a date cannot be verified, mark it `Unknown`.

Treat a cluster of recent creation signals as a major risk factor. The highest-risk launch-only footprint is: a recently registered domain, a new or recently repurposed X account whose relevant posts began within days, a newly deployed token, an anonymous team with no delivery history, and no credible independent developers, users, investors, or established accounts acknowledging the project. Absence of a large KOL alone is not evidence of fraud, but absence of any independent reputation or organic activity removes an important corroborating signal.

A polished website is not product evidence. Look for AI/template-shell indicators such as generic or internally inconsistent copy, copied legal text, placeholder links, non-functional product flows, demo-only data, unverifiable dashboards/testimonials/partners, cloned layouts, stock or generated team imagery, and a roadmap or documentation created at the same time as the token. AI use itself is not misconduct; the risk is a rapidly generated presentation layer with no independently verifiable product, users, revenue, code history, or operator history.

When multiple freshness and template-shell signals co-occur, say plainly that the project may be an AI-generated launch wrapper designed to sell a token and has a high short-life/rug probability. Do not soften this to “early stage” unless there is affirmative evidence of real development. Still reserve definitive labels such as `scam`, `rug`, or `fraud` for transaction, deception, identity, or abandonment evidence that supports them.

## X compromise signals

Look for clusters: abrupt handle/avatar/language/topic changes, unrelated older posts, mass deletion, long dormancy followed by token promotion, sudden domain/CA replacement, warnings from previously linked identities, or current control conflicting with older attestations. Classify as `no material signal observed`, `suspicious pivot/rename`, `probable compromise`, or `confirmed compromise`. Use the last two only with strong corroboration. New, small, or anonymous is unverified—not automatically hacked.

Label team status as `doxxed and corroborated`, `pseudonymous with history`, `anonymous`, or `misrepresented`. Company registration alone does not connect the entity to the exact token.

## Community and meme voice

Separate followers, token holders, active users, paying users, and community participants. For Meme evaluation, examine:

- organic exact-ticker/CA mentions from accounts other than the official account;
- unique recurring participants and original memes/remixes, not copied raid text;
- engagement distribution rather than dependence on one KOL or a few farm accounts;
- cross-platform spread, recognizability outside the token community, and language/geographic breadth;
- continuity through drawdowns, community-led activity, and transparent incident response;
- bot-like timing, repeated templates, giveaway-only engagement, purchased followers, and coordinated shilling.

Assess the meme itself: pre-existing cultural recognition, crypto-native resonance, clarity, remixability, originality, and narrative longevity. A loud but purely manufactured launch campaign has community volume, not necessarily community voice.

When social search is incomplete, lower confidence rather than inventing reach. For very new tokens, state the observation window and sample-size limitation.
